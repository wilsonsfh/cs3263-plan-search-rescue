FIELD DISPATCHES (several sources; listed in the order they reached the control room)
[06:45] Radio Nuwakot FM (local radio, early bulletin): Debris is blocking the Khanigaun–Kispang road. Heavy rain is forecast to continue through the evening. Around 4 people are believed to be stranded in Samundratar (unconfirmed estimate). Around 4 people are believed to be stranded in Meghang (unconfirmed estimate). The Meghang–Salme road is still passable this morning, drivers say.
[13:15] Sunita Lama, The Himalayan Times (on the ground): Confirmed headcount: Residents at the health post in Samundratar say two adults are still there, sheltering on upper floors. The Khanigaun–Kispang road has been reopened after bulldozers removed the debris; travel times are back to normal. Our team counted one injured person (an elderly woman with a broken hip), six schoolchildren and one able-bodied adult at the school in Likhu, all needing evacuation. Confirmed headcount: Our team counted one injured person (an elderly woman with a broken hip), two elderly residents and four uninjured adults at the health post in Meghang, all needing evacuation. Floodwater has turned the road from Meghang to Salme into a river, cutting a road that was open earlier today. At the temple in Tupche, an old man and one uninjured adult are waiting to be rescued. The Salme–Samundratar road is open but badly waterlogged; driving it adds a quarter of an hour to the usual journey. A government helicopter was seen flying north this morning but did not land.
[09:30] District Police Office (situation report): The Kispang–Meghang road is open but badly waterlogged; driving it takes twice the usual time. Debris has completely choked the road between Kispang and Salme. Livestock losses across the district are reported to be severe.
Anything not mentioned in these dispatches is in its usual condition, and no one else is known to be stranded.
