MISSION SHEET
- Team base and starting point: Chaughada
- Shelter where evacuees must be delivered: Chaughada
- Vehicle capacity: 17 people at a time
- Places on the district map: Chaughada (evacuation point), Battar (temple), Madanpur (village), Lachyang (temple), Chhap (health post), Dupcheshwar (village)

- Battar–Chaughada: 25 min
- Battar–Chhap: 45 min
- Battar–Dupcheshwar: 40 min
- Chaughada–Chhap: 20 min
- Chaughada–Dupcheshwar: 45 min
- Chaughada–Madanpur: 15 min
- Chhap–Lachyang: 10 min
- Dupcheshwar–Madanpur: 20 min

FIELD DISPATCHES (several sources; listed in the order they reached the control room)
[06:45] Radio Nuwakot FM (local radio, early bulletin): The Home Ministry says relief funds will be released next week. Residents of Dupcheshwar were seen on rooftops last night. Debris is blocking the Battar–Chhap road. Debris is blocking the Chaughada–Madanpur road. The Chaughada–Chhap road is still passable this morning, drivers say. Around 6 people are believed to be stranded in Lachyang (unconfirmed estimate). Around 4 people are believed to be stranded in Madanpur (unconfirmed estimate). Around 12 people are believed to be stranded in Chhap (unconfirmed estimate).
[09:30] District Police Office (situation report): Mobile networks remain patchy across the district. The only bridge linking Battar and Chaughada has collapsed.
[13:15] Sunita Lama, The Himalayan Times (on the ground): Confirmed headcount: At the temple in Lachyang, three children and one uninjured adult are waiting to be rescued. The Chaughada–Chhap road is under two metres of fast-flowing floodwater, cutting a road that was open earlier today. The Chhap–Lachyang road is open but badly waterlogged; driving it takes twice the usual time. Confirmed headcount: At the health post in Chhap, two injured people (a teenage girl with a fractured arm and a child with a deep gash on his leg), four schoolchildren and four able-bodied adults are waiting to be rescued. Confirmed headcount: At Madanpur, one child, an elderly couple and 3 other adults are waiting to be rescued. Everyone from Dupcheshwar has already been airlifted out. Residents at the temple in Battar say three injured people (a man with a crushed foot, an old man with a head wound and a young boy with a badly sprained ankle), four schoolchildren, one senior citizen and five uninjured adults are still there, sheltering on upper floors. The Battar–Chhap road has been reopened after bulldozers removed the debris; travel times are back to normal. Army engineers have cleared the debris on the Chaughada–Madanpur road; it is open again at normal speed. Heavy rain is forecast to continue through the evening. The Battar–Dupcheshwar road is open but badly waterlogged; driving it takes twice the usual time.
Anything not mentioned in these dispatches is in its usual condition, and no one else is known to be stranded.
