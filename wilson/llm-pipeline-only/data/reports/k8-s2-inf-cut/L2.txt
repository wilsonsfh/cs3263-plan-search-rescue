FIELD DISPATCH (Prakash Thapa, Kantipur correspondent, filed 12:10)
A landslide has buried the road between Ramche and Thansing under metres of mud. The suspension bridge on the Chaughada–Thansing route was swept away by the river. Residents at Kumari say three children and six able-bodied adults are still there, sheltering on upper floors. Our team counted one child, three elderly residents and six other adults at Ramche, all needing evacuation. Debris has completely choked the road between Bhalche and Chaughada. Everyone from Likhu has already been airlifted out. Army engineers have cleared the debris on the Bhalche–Taruka road; it is open again at normal speed. The suspension bridge on the Bidur–Taruka route was swept away by the river. At the ward office in Madanpur, a baby, one senior citizen and four other adults are waiting to be rescued. The Kumari–Ramche road is muddy and frightening to drive, but our jeep got through in the usual time. The Bhalche–Madanpur road is open but badly waterlogged; driving it adds 15 minutes to the usual journey. The Dhaibung–Likhu road is open but badly waterlogged; driving it adds 20 minutes to the usual journey. Debris has completely choked the road between Dhaibung and Kumari. Our team counted four children, two elderly residents and 5 uninjured adults at Chaughada, all needing evacuation. The Bidur–Madanpur road is open but badly waterlogged; driving it takes twice the usual time. Our team counted an old man and five other adults at the ward office in Bhalche, all needing evacuation. Stranded at the health post in Dhaibung: two adults. The only bridge linking Bidur and Chaughada has collapsed. At Taruka, 3 injured people (a woman with burns on her arms, a young boy with a badly sprained ankle and a child with a deep gash on his leg), three schoolchildren and five able-bodied adults are waiting to be rescued. Residents at the school in Bidur say one injured person (a teenage girl with a fractured arm), five children and two other adults are still there, sheltering on upper floors. Apart from what is described here, roads are in their usual condition and no one else is known to be stranded.
