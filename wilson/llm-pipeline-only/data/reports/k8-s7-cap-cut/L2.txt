FIELD DISPATCH (Prakash Thapa, Kantipur correspondent, filed 12:10)
Our team counted three injured people (a teenage girl with a fractured arm, a pregnant woman who was hit by debris and a young boy with a badly sprained ankle) and 2 other adults at Shikharbesi, all needing evacuation. Stranded at the temple in Ramche: one injured person (an elderly woman with a broken hip), two elderly residents and two able-bodied adults. Stranded at Goljung: four children and six able-bodied adults. The Dupcheshwar–Meghang road is open but badly waterlogged; driving it adds a quarter of an hour to the usual journey. Floodwater has turned the road from Goljung to Kumari into a river. The Meghang–Shikharbesi road has been reopened after bulldozers removed the debris; travel times are back to normal. Hillside collapse: the Dupcheshwar–Rautbesi road disappeared under a landslide overnight. The Dhaibung–Ramche road is under two metres of fast-flowing floodwater. The suspension bridge on the Kumari–Ramche route was swept away by the river. The Haku–Shikharbesi road is open but badly waterlogged; driving it takes twice the usual time. Kalikasthan was fully evacuated by the army yesterday; no one remains there. Stranded at the health post in Kumari: two children and three uninjured adults. Our team counted an elderly couple and five uninjured adults at Haku, all needing evacuation. The Dupcheshwar–Kalikasthan road is open but badly waterlogged; driving it takes twice the usual time. The only bridge linking Dupcheshwar and Goljung has collapsed. Army engineers have cleared the debris on the Rautbesi–Shikharbesi road; it is open again at normal speed. At the school in Meghang, nine schoolchildren and one able-bodied adult are waiting to be rescued. Residents at Dhaibung say one schoolchild, an old man and five uninjured adults are still there, sheltering on upper floors. Residents at the temple in Dupcheshwar say 2 children, an old man and two other adults are still there, sheltering on upper floors. Apart from what is described here, roads are in their usual condition and no one else is known to be stranded.
