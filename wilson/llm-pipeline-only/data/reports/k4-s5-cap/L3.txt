MISSION SHEET
- Team base and starting point: Taruka
- Shelter where evacuees must be delivered: Taruka
- Vehicle capacity: 17 people at a time
- Places on the district map: Taruka (evacuation point), Thaprek (village), Suryagadhi (village), Chaughada (village), Likhu (temple), Samundratar (village), Sundaradevi (road junction)

FIELD DISPATCHES (several sources; listed in the order they reached the control room)
[06:45] Radio Nuwakot FM (local radio, early bulletin): Around 10 people are believed to be stranded in Likhu (unconfirmed estimate). Around 8 people are believed to be stranded in Thaprek (unconfirmed estimate). Around 4 people are believed to be stranded in Suryagadhi (unconfirmed estimate). A government helicopter was seen flying north this morning but did not land. Debris is blocking the Chaughada–Likhu road. Residents of Samundratar were seen on rooftops last night.
[09:30] District Police Office (situation report): Our team counted two children, an old man and one uninjured adult at Chaughada, all needing evacuation. The Chaughada–Thaprek road is open but badly waterlogged; driving it now takes double the normal journey time. Livestock losses across the district are reported to be severe. The Likhu–Sundaradevi road is open but badly waterlogged; driving it takes twice the usual time. Floodwater has turned the road from Suryagadhi to Taruka into a river. The only bridge linking Taruka and Thaprek has collapsed.
[13:15] Sunita Lama, The Himalayan Times (on the ground): Confirmed headcount: At the temple in Likhu, three schoolchildren, 3 elderly residents and two other adults are waiting to be rescued. Samundratar was fully evacuated by the army yesterday; no one remains there. Confirmed headcount: Residents at Thaprek say one injured person (a teenage girl with a fractured arm), two children, an elderly couple and five uninjured adults are still there, sheltering on upper floors. Confirmed headcount: Stranded at Suryagadhi: an elderly couple and five able-bodied adults. The Chaughada–Likhu road has been reopened after bulldozers removed the debris; travel times are back to normal. In Kathmandu, schools have been closed for three days as a precaution.
Anything not mentioned in these dispatches is in its usual condition, and no one else is known to be stranded.
