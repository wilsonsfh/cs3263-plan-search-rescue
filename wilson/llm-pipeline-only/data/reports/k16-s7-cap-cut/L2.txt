FIELD DISPATCH (Prakash Thapa, Kantipur correspondent, filed 12:10)
The Kalikasthan–Sundaradevi road has been reopened after bulldozers removed the debris; travel times are back to normal. The Kalikasthan–Panchakanya road is open but badly waterlogged; driving it now takes double the normal journey time. Boulders and fallen trees make the Rautbesi–Tupche road impassable for any vehicle. The suspension bridge on the Likhu–Sundaradevi route was swept away by the river. The Goljung–Panchakanya road is under two metres of fast-flowing floodwater. Our team counted three schoolchildren, an old man and two other adults at the temple in Betrawati, all needing evacuation. The only bridge linking Betrawati and Chaughada has collapsed. The Bhalche–Goljung road is open but badly waterlogged; driving it adds 10 minutes to the usual journey. Army engineers have cleared the debris on the Ghyangphedi–Rautbesi road; it is open again at normal speed. Residents at the temple in Trishuli say 4 children, one senior citizen and 3 able-bodied adults are still there, sheltering on upper floors. The Rautbesi–Salme road is open but badly waterlogged; driving it adds 10 minutes to the usual journey. Our team counted one schoolchild, an old man and four uninjured adults at the temple in Chaughada, all needing evacuation. The Bidur–Gerkhu road is open but badly waterlogged; driving it now takes double the normal journey time. At Samundratar, 3 injured people (a woman with burns on her arms, a child with a deep gash on his leg and an elderly woman with a broken hip), two schoolchildren, three elderly residents and three uninjured adults are waiting to be rescued. Residents at the temple in Gerkhu say an old man and five uninjured adults are still there, sheltering on upper floors. The Belkot–Betrawati road is under two metres of fast-flowing floodwater. Our team counted one schoolchild, an elderly couple and 4 other adults at Salme, all needing evacuation. Boulders and fallen trees make the Goljung–Tupche road impassable for any vehicle. The Sundaradevi–Trishuli road is open but badly waterlogged; driving it now takes double the normal journey time. Our team counted 2 children, two senior citizens and one able-bodied adult at Belkot, all needing evacuation. Residents at the temple in Kalikasthan say one child and five other adults are still there, sheltering on upper floors. Army engineers have cleared the debris on the Chaughada–Lachyang road; it is open again at normal speed. Stranded at the school in Bhalche: 5 schoolchildren, two senior citizens and one uninjured adult. Our team counted two schoolchildren, two senior citizens and one able-bodied adult at Tupche, all needing evacuation. Boulders and fallen trees make the Ghyangphedi–Tupche road impassable for any vehicle. Army engineers have cleared the debris on the Bhalche–Bhorle road; it is open again at normal speed. Our team counted two injured people (a man with a crushed foot and a young boy with a badly sprained ankle), one schoolchild, one senior citizen and two able-bodied adults at the health post in Panchakanya, all needing evacuation. At the health post in Sundaradevi, three injured people (a young boy with a badly sprained ankle, a pregnant woman who was hit by debris and a woman with burns on her arms), two children and 5 uninjured adults are waiting to be rescued. Residents at Bhorle say 4 children and five uninjured adults are still there, sheltering on upper floors. Stranded at Rautbesi: one senior citizen and five able-bodied adults. Our team counted three injured people (a man with a crushed foot, a young boy with a badly sprained ankle and a pregnant woman who was hit by debris), 2 elderly residents and six able-bodied adults at the health post in Bidur, all needing evacuation. Residents at the temple in Likhu say three injured people (a man with a crushed foot, a woman with burns on her arms and a child with a deep gash on his leg), an elderly couple and one able-bodied adult are still there, sheltering on upper floors. The suspension bridge on the Likhu–Panchakanya route was swept away by the river. Apart from what is described here, roads are in their usual condition and no one else is known to be stranded.
