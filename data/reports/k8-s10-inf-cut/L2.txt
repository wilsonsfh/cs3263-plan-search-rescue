FIELD DISPATCH (Prakash Thapa, Kantipur correspondent, filed 12:10)
Army engineers have cleared the debris on the Goljung–Samundratar road; it is open again at normal speed. Residents at the temple in Samundratar say two injured people (an elderly woman with a broken hip and a teenage girl with a fractured arm), 2 schoolchildren, one elderly resident and 1 able-bodied adult are still there, sheltering on upper floors. The Dupcheshwar–Taruka road is open but badly waterlogged; driving it takes twice the usual time. The Battar–Samundratar road is open but badly waterlogged; driving it adds 20 minutes to the usual journey. Residents at the school in Dupcheshwar say 3 schoolchildren and one other adult are still there, sheltering on upper floors. The only bridge linking Haku and Ramche has collapsed. At Madanpur, a baby, an old man and four other adults are waiting to be rescued. At the school in Dandagaun, seven schoolchildren, three elderly residents and two able-bodied adults are waiting to be rescued. Our team counted one injured person (a man with a crushed foot), two children and 5 other adults at the temple in Belkot, all needing evacuation. Stranded at Battar: four schoolchildren, 3 elderly residents and one other adult. At Ramche, one injured person (a woman with burns on her arms), one child and five able-bodied adults are waiting to be rescued. Boulders and fallen trees make the Dandagaun–Dupcheshwar road impassable for any vehicle. Locals warned us about the Dandagaun–Madanpur road, yet it is intact and passable at normal speed. Residents at the health post in Salme say four children, three elderly residents and five other adults are still there, sheltering on upper floors. The Bhalche–Likhu road is under two metres of fast-flowing floodwater. Everyone from Bhalche has already been airlifted out. Everyone from Goljung has already been airlifted out. The Belkot–Bhalche road is open but badly waterlogged; driving it adds 10 minutes to the usual journey. Debris has completely choked the road between Bhalche and Taruka. The Bhalche–Goljung road has been reopened after bulldozers removed the debris; travel times are back to normal. Apart from what is described here, roads are in their usual condition and no one else is known to be stranded.
