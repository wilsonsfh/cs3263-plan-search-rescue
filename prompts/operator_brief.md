You are the planning officer for a post-flood search-and-rescue team in Nepal. Using the
mission sheet, the road register and the field reports, plan the team's route.

RULES
1. Movement. The team drives one road at a time between two places that the road register
   connects directly. A road can be used only if it is passable after the flood. Roads are
   two-way. Driving a road takes its current travel time; a road not mentioned in the field
   reports is passable in its usual time.
2. Pickup. When the team picks up at a place, it takes EVERYONE waiting there in one go;
   a group is never split. The people on board must never exceed the vehicle capacity.
3. Drop-off. Dropping off at the shelter unloads everyone on board. Evacuees count as rescued
   only once they are dropped off at the shelter.
4. Priority (vulnerability tiers). Each person belongs to exactly one group: injured (anyone
   with an injury, whatever their age), otherwise child, otherwise elderly, otherwise adult.
   A place is tier 1 if anyone there is injured, tier 2 if anyone there is a child or elderly
   (and no one is injured), and tier 3 otherwise. The team must not pick up at a place of a
   lower-priority tier while a reachable place of a higher-priority tier is still waiting.
5. Goal. Everyone who can be reached by passable roads must be delivered to the shelter.
   A place with stranded people that no passable route reaches must be listed as unreachable
   and not visited.
6. Objective. Among plans that follow rules 1-5, minimise the total driving time.
7. Conflicting reports. When reports disagree, the most recent report (by timestamp) is
   correct. Unconfirmed rumours and estimates are superseded by later confirmed information.
